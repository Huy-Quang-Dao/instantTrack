How it works

One loop. Rebuilt for the GPU.

InstantTrack keeps SuperMimic’s loop: one world model, analytic policy gradients through it. It makes every step of the loop cheaper, starts the policy from the reference pose (v1.1), and trains both networks with Muon (v1.2).

Failed clips

Fifteen or more. At most one.

After 6 minutes of training, SuperMimic and SuperMimic + EMA still fail 15 or more of the 40 clips, every fall and get-up among them. InstantTrack v1.1 fails 3 to 7, mostly falls, get-ups and dances. InstantTrack v1.2 fails none in four of five seeds and one in the fifth.3

What changed.

Rotations that the compiler can fuse

315 → 209 ms

per training iteration. The 3×3 rotation products in kinematics, reward and world model are written element-wise, and reward, observation and goal are compiled. Kernel launches per iteration drop from 25.3k to 15.2k.4

A right-sized world model

+28%

iterations per second. For 40 clips, a 1024³ world model learns as much per iteration as the paper’s 2048³.

An averaged policy

2.7×

on SuperMimic alone. Polyak-averaged policy weights take SuperMimic from 1.50 h to 0.55 h, so we count the EMA as part of the baseline.

Resets aimed at failures

0.27 → 0.22 h

mean time to the target. 30% of resets start one second before where episodes recently failed, so hard clips stop flickering between checkpoints.

New in v1.2: Muon, CUDA graphs and a smaller policy

0.19 → 0.12 h

mean time to the target. Muon orthogonalizes the updates of the hidden layers of the world model and the policy, and the world model fits better. Both update phases replay as CUDA graphs, and the policy is smaller (1024-512-256). Iterations to the target fall to about 3.3k, and iterations per second rise from 6.3 to 8.7. The smaller policy is a size choice: with the original policy, the other changes give 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.10 h on three seeds.

v1.1: a policy that starts from the reference

0.25 → 0.19 h

mean time to the target. The policy outputs a correction on top of the next reference joint pose instead of an offset from the standing pose, so an untrained policy already follows the motion. Iterations to the target drop from about 6k to 3.7k–5.8k, at the same speed per iteration.

A contact reward in imagination

0.74 → 0.81

foot-contact agreement with the reference. The feet are rewarded for matching the reference contact state and for not sliding while it is down. Evaluation keeps SuperMimic’s reward.

Questions? Answers.

Is this the official SuperMimic code?

No. No official SuperMimic code is released. Our SuperMimic is the mjlab port of supermimic_vibed, an independent reimplementation of the paper. The paper reports about 1.2 h to return 0.82 on an H100; we measure 1.50 h on an RTX 5090, under a stricter criterion. Every speed-up on this page compares InstantTrack with our SuperMimic on the same machine, not with the paper’s numbers.

Why compare with SuperMimic + EMA?

A Polyak-averaged policy alone takes SuperMimic from 1.50 h to 0.55 h, and any SuperMimic implementation can adopt it. So the fair baseline is SuperMimic + EMA, and the headline is 4.7×, not 13×.

What do the 7 minutes include?

The full training loop on an otherwise idle GPU: simulation, world-model fitting, policy and critic updates, torch.compile warm-up, CUDA-graph capture and checkpoint writing. The 7 minutes are the mean of five seeds: four take 6 minutes, one takes 11. Checkpoint evaluation runs afterwards and is not counted.

What counts as reaching the target?

Normalized return ≥ 0.82 with 0 of 40 clips failed, on 3 checkpoints in a row, so a single lucky checkpoint does not count. Checkpoints come every 3 minutes, so times are quantized to 0.05 h. The v1.2 runs saved one every 1.5 minutes; we score them on the same 3-minute schedule, and every checkpoint gives the same times.

Where is InstantTrack worse?

Global root drift is larger: 52 cm against 50 cm at 1.5 h, because the feet do not slide with the reference. Hard clips can come back after the target is first met: in one v1.2 seed, a jump fails again at two checkpoints, which pushes that seed from 0.12 to 0.18 h. At 1.5 h v1.1 keeps slightly lower pose errors than v1.2. Without the contact reward, InstantTrack v1.0 is better on every metric we measure, at 0.890 return.

Is the smaller policy a method?

No. It is a network-size choice, a hyperparameter, and larger problems may need a larger policy. With SuperMimic’s policy size, Muon, CUDA graphs and batched goal encoding give 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.10 h on three seeds, against 0.10 / 0.10 / 0.10 h with the smaller policy and 0.15 / 0.15 / 0.16 h for v1.1. Without Muon, the smaller policy does not help.

What is not covered?

Only the Unitree G1 in simulation, on LAFAN1’s 40 clips. There are no evaluation-time pushes or randomization, no larger motion library and no real-robot experiment. Plain SuperMimic has one seed, InstantTrack v1.1 and v1.2 five, the other methods three. v1.2 accuracy is measured on 3 seeds at 0.5 h and on one run at 1.5 h.

What did not help?

On plain SuperMimic: a smaller policy and world model, demonstration replay, policy batch 2048, EMA 0.999, lr 6e-4, horizons 16 and 24, a foot-contact world-model loss, anchored imagination, more critic epochs, UTD 0.5, CUDA graphs, world-model window 4, a contact-mode world model and, for v1.1, a wide-kernel root reward, a world-model ensemble with a disagreement penalty and a mixture-of-experts policy, and for v1.2 a SimBa residual policy, spectral normalization of the world model, Muon on only one network, and lr 6e-4. Each is in the README with its run logs.
