How it works

One loop. Rebuilt for the GPU.

InstantTrack keeps SuperMimic’s loop: one world model, analytic policy gradients through it. It makes every step of the loop cheaper.

Failed clips

Twelve. Ten. None.

After 15 minutes of training, SuperMimic + EMA still fails 9 to 12 of the 40 clips and SuperMimic fails 10, most of them falls and get-ups. InstantTrack fails none, in every seed.3

What changed.

Rotations that the compiler can fuse

315 → 209 ms

per training iteration. The 3×3 rotation products in kinematics, reward and world model are written element-wise, and reward, observation and goal are compiled. Kernel launches per iteration drop from 25.3k to 15.2k.4

A right-sized world model

+28%

iterations per second. For 40 clips, a 1024³ world model learns as much per iteration as the paper’s 2048³.

An averaged policy

2.7×

on SuperMimic alone. Polyak-averaged policy weights take SuperMimic from 1.50 h to 0.55 h, so we count the EMA as part of the baseline.

Resets aimed at failures

0.27 → 0.22 h

mean time to the target. 30% of resets start one second before where episodes recently failed, so hard clips stop flickering between checkpoints.

A contact reward in imagination

0.74 → 0.81

foot-contact agreement with the reference. The feet are rewarded for matching the reference contact state and for not sliding while it is down. Evaluation keeps SuperMimic’s reward.

Questions? Answers.

Is this the official SuperMimic code?

No. No official SuperMimic code is released. Our SuperMimic is the mjlab port of supermimic_vibed, an independent reimplementation of the paper. The paper reports about 1.2 h to return 0.82 on an H100; we measure 1.50 h on an RTX 5090, under a stricter criterion. Every speed-up on this page compares InstantTrack with our SuperMimic on the same machine, not with the paper’s numbers.

Why compare with SuperMimic + EMA?

A Polyak-averaged policy alone takes SuperMimic from 1.50 h to 0.55 h, and any SuperMimic implementation can adopt it. So the fair baseline is SuperMimic + EMA, and the headline is 2.2×, not 6×.

What do the 15 minutes include?

The full training loop on an otherwise idle GPU: simulation, world-model fitting, policy and critic updates, torch.compile warm-up and checkpoint writing. Checkpoint evaluation runs afterwards and is not counted.

What counts as reaching the target?

Normalized return ≥ 0.82 with 0 of 40 clips failed, on 3 checkpoints in a row, so a single lucky checkpoint does not count. Checkpoints come every 3 minutes, so times are quantized to 0.05 h.

Where is InstantTrack worse?

With the contact reward, normalized return at 1.5 h is 0.877, against 0.880 for SuperMimic + EMA, and global root drift is larger. Without the contact reward, InstantTrack is better on every metric we measure, at 0.890 return.

What is not covered?

Only the Unitree G1 in simulation, on LAFAN1’s 40 clips. There are no evaluation-time pushes or randomization, no larger motion library and no real-robot experiment. Plain SuperMimic has one seed; the other methods have three.

What did not help?

A smaller policy, demonstration replay, policy batch 2048, EMA 0.999, lr 6e-4, horizons 16 and 24, a foot-contact world-model loss, anchored imagination, more critic epochs, UTD 0.5, CUDA graphs, world-model window 4 and a contact-mode world model. Each is in the README with its run logs.

  1. Normalized return is a clip’s return divided by the maximum possible over the whole clip, averaged over the 40 clips, from 0 to 1. All 40 clips play once from their first frame, with friction 1.0, no randomization or noise, and a deterministic policy.
  2. Training wall-clock on one RTX 5090 with 4096 parallel environments; evaluation is not counted.
  3. Checkpoint nearest to 0.25 h of each run. A clip fails if the rollout triggers SuperMimic’s training termination (root height or tilt error).
